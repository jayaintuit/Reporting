Additional insights for leadership

Patterns beyond the core questions that are worth flagging.

A. Connections and traffic are concentrated at the extremes

Apps with 100+ connections are just 0.2% of all apps but hold 22.8% of all active connections. Meanwhile the 1–5 tier alone drives 84.6% of total API call volume, meaning platform load is dominated by low-connection apps, not the largest ones.

B. Reliability is roughly flat across scale, with the smallest tier worst

Average error rate is highest in the 1–5 tier (4.72%) and lowest in the 100+ tier (3.46%), with the middle tiers close together (3.7–3.8%). Smaller/newer apps carry modestly more error risk than established ones.

C. Retention rises sharply with connection count

Share of apps "still active 60+ days" climbs from 55.8% in the 1–5 tier to 78.4% in the 100+ tier. Apps that reach scale are far stickier — early-stage, low-connection apps are where most churn risk concentrates.

D. Per-connection call intensity is driven by outliers

Mean calls-per-connection is highest in the smallest bucket (22,236 for 1–5 vs. 1,100 for 100+), but the median tells a different story (592 vs. 192) — a handful of single-connection apps (internal ops/automation tools) generate extreme call volumes and pull the average up. Treat 1–5-tier "average usage" figures with caution.

E. Writes fail 8.4x more often than reads

Read calls error out 5.1% of the time; write calls error out 42.5% of the time. That gap is large enough to be a genuine developer-experience problem on the write surface (validation rules, required fields, sparse-update conflicts, duplicate detection) rather than noise.

F. Tier distribution is flat despite scale

95.9% of all apps sit on the BUILDER tier regardless of connection count, including many 100+-connection apps. Connection scale is not translating into partner tier progression today — a possible gap in tier/monetization alignment worth a closer look.
