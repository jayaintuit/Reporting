Where apps land on read/write mix

query_<entity> — a read/search call reports_<name> — a canned financial report (read-only) bare entity name (e.g. invoice, purchase, bill) — a write: create, update, or void
invoice and query_invoice are the two single biggest entities in the ecosystem — invoice-related traffic alone is an estimated ~25% of all calls (create/update + query combined). reports_profitandloss is the single most common #1 entity of all, called by 13.7% of apps as their primary use case — more than any transactional entity — confirming financial reporting/BI is a bigger driver of app traffic than transaction sync.

We validated the query_/reports_/bare-name convention against actual write volume: apps whose top entity is query_ or reports_ are read_only_no_writes 48–82% of the time with near-zero writes, while apps whose top entity is a bare name are mixed_read_write 46.5% of the time with real write volume (thousands of successful writes/app on average). One exception: companyinfo appears bare but is a read-only metadata lookup in practice, not a real write target.

Job-to-be-done: why apps connect to QBO

Classified business purpose of the 25,652 apps, platform-wide.

Writes fail 8x more often than reads

Read calls error out 4.9% of the time; write calls error out 38.4% of the time. That gap is large enough to be a genuine developer-experience problem on the write surface (validation rules, required fields, sparse-update conflicts, duplicate detection) rather than noise — worth a closer look from API/DX teams.

Payments & E-commerce (28.1%), BI & Analytics (19.4%), Bill Pay & AP (19.0%), and Accounting & Bookkeeping (14.7%) account for over 80% of all apps — directly mirroring the entity data above (invoice/payment traffic, reports_* traffic, and purchase/bill/vendor traffic respectively). The ecosystem skews toward apps that read financial data out of QBO for reporting, sync, and dashboards, more than apps that write transactions in.

Additional insights for leadership

Patterns beyond the three core connection-bucket questions that are worth flagging.
Patterns beyond the three core questions that are worth flagging.

A. Connections and traffic are concentrated at the extremes

Apps with 100+ connections are just 0.2% of all apps but hold 18.6% of all active connections — nearly as much as the 24,551 apps in the 1–5 tier hold per-app-adjusted share. Meanwhile the 1–5 tier alone drives 85.5% of total API call volume, meaning platform load is dominated by low-connection apps, not the largest ones.

B. Reliability improves with scale

Average error rate falls steadily as connection count grows: 4.77% (1–5) → 3.61% → 3.15% → 2.50% (100+). Larger, more established apps are meaningfully more stable, which is worth factoring into any support or SLA prioritization.

C. Retention rises sharply with connection count

Share of apps "still active 60+ days" climbs from 47.5% in the 1–5 tier to 82.1% in the 100+ tier. Apps that reach scale are far stickier — early-stage, low-connection apps are where most churn risk concentrates.

D. Per-connection call intensity is driven by outliers

Mean calls-per-connection is highest in the smallest bucket (16,357 for 1–5 vs. 1,108 for 100+), but the median tells a different story (449 vs. 163) — a handful of single-connection apps (internal ops/automation tools) generate extreme call volumes and pull the average up. Treat 1–5-tier "average usage" figures with caution.

E. Use case mix shifts toward BI & reporting at scale

"BI & Analytics" apps rise from 18.4% of the 1–5 tier to 51.3% of the 100+ tier, mirroring the reports_profitandloss trend above. Smaller apps are more evenly spread across Payments, Bill Pay/AP, Accounting, and CRM use cases; large-scale integrations skew heavily toward reporting/analytics.

F. Tier distribution is flat despite scale

92–96% of apps sit on the BUILDER tier in every connection bucket, including the 100+ tier. Connection scale is not translating into partner tier progression today — a possible gap in tier/monetization alignment worth a closer look.
